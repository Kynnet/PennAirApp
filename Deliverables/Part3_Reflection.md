# Approach

The final pipeline uses three motion cues and minimal texture cues.
Although my first attempts included using the texture std measurement from part 1, I realized that the background was not noisy enough for this to accurately work, and the shapes themselves also had added noise.

What makes this video tractable is that the camera never moves, so the background is essentially fixed. Background subtraction with MOG2 gives a far cleaner silhouette than the texture bitmask does, but the two cues fail in different places, so the mask actually used is their union. The texture bitmask is anchored to the motion mask so that it only fills in the parts of a shape the model missed rather than firing on smooth stretches of road, and the same standard deviation image is reused afterwards as a veto that throws out blobs no smoother than the road around them.

Optical flow does the tracking work, using only OpenCV implementations. Sparse Lucas-Kanade on features inside a shape gives that shape's velocity, which predicts where it will be in the next frame. Dense Farneback flow runs on the bounding box of any blob that looks like two shapes stuck together, since two overlapping shapes are one region in space but two clusters in the flow field, and k-means on the flow vectors separates them.

For the frames where segmentation cannot help at all, each track re-finds its own outline by correlating its remembered silhouette against the bitmask. This still works while shapes overlap, because an occluded shape's pixels are still foreground and the occluder is merely standing on them.

Shapes are named from the outline alone. Circularity separates circles, approxPolyDP counts vertices, and quadrilaterals are split into square, rectangle, trapezoid or parallelogram using their interior angles and parallel sides. The polygon that gets drawn is fitted to the number of edges the name implies, so the outline and the label can never disagree.

I also tried making optical flow the basis of the whole pipeline rather than just the tracking. Flow fields are smooth by construction, so a flow mask is a halo rather than a silhouette, and naming shapes from one agreed with the background model only about a fifth of the time. Flow also cannot see a shape that has momentarily stopped moving. It turns out to be the right tool for correspondence between frames and the wrong tool for finding a boundary.

# Challenges

Speed was the first problem. The initial working version used a hand written rolling temporal median for the background and whole frame Farneback flow, which cost 114 ms and 83 ms per frame respectively and ran at 14 fps. Replacing the median with MOG2 at 2.6 ms, restricting dense flow to bounding boxes, batching every track's Lucas-Kanade points into one call and only relocating outlines when a detection could not explain them brought it to 34 fps with better accuracy.

MOG2 measures and learns in the same call, which caused a subtle bug. The model must not learn the shapes it is tracking, or a shape that slows down is absorbed into the background and disappears. Hiding tracked shapes behind the model's own background image stops them entering its history, but it also erases them from the returned mask, leaving every tracked shape to be found by the texture cue alone. The fix is to call apply twice, once with a learning rate of zero to measure and once on the protected frame purely to update.

Thresholds that outlive the component they were tuned against caused the most damage. A flatness test rejects blobs that look like background, and it was set at 0.85 back when the background model was prone to leaving ghosts. The white trapezoid scores 0.86, because its gradient is bright enough that compression noise across it rivals the asphalt grain, so it was being silently deleted from every frame. Moving that one number to 0.95 took recall from 0.79 to 0.91. Removing the test altogether was worse than either, so it earns its place, just not that low.

Overlapping shapes were the hardest part conceptually. Concavity is the obvious sign that two shapes have run into each other, but it is unreliable, since a circle resting on a triangle produces a perfectly convex blob. Three things were needed: asking the existing tracks whether two of them are standing inside one blob that is bigger than either, asking the flow field whether the two halves are moving differently, and, where neither answers, labelling the region "merged shapes" instead of inventing a name for it. Earlier versions confidently called a circle on a triangle a trapezoid, and drawing an outline in the right place is useful while naming it is fabrication.

The worst bug was one a track could never recover from. Each track decided whether to trust a new detection by comparing it against its own remembered area, which sounds reasonable and has no way back. A single bad silhouette, such as a merged pair or a shape caught halfway off the frame edge, writes a wrong remembered area, and from then on the correct detection disagrees with that wrong memory and is refused for disagreeing. Instrumenting it found one track locked out for 129 consecutive frames, more than four seconds of drawing a stale outline under the wrong name while a perfectly good detection sat underneath it every frame. Comparing against how big the shapes in the clip actually are, rather than against the track's own memory, fixed it.

Chasing the outlines that still looked wrong turned up two more versions of the same mistake, and all of them collapse into one rule: a remembered outline may only be preferred to a fresh measurement when it still fits the image and there is something present with room to hide a shape inside. Without the first half, tracks redraw stale templates while their containment quietly decays. Without the second half, a lost track parks its outline on an ordinary shape another track already owns.

Several changes that seemed obviously correct measured worse and were reverted, which is why the evaluation mattered more than any single idea. Deduplicating tracks by containment rather than overlap looks sharper but deletes small shapes passing behind large ones. Refusing to associate a detection whose name or Hu moments disagree with a track sounds safe but makes settled tracks reject their own shape on the frames where the silhouette is briefly imperfect, tripling identity churn. Almost every real defect was found by instrumenting the code rather than by watching the output, since on screen they all looked like the same generic tracking wobble.
